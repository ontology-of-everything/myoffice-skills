# Organize + Distinguish

- Organize the main point, key content, important concepts, opinions, and factual claims, preserving
  the central argument and its conditions.

- Distinguish factual claims, opinions/inferences, feelings, and experience, labeling evidence as
  supported, contested, or unverified; source reports are not independent verification, and
  experience is not a general rule.

- Separately summarize highly liked content, agreement within the material, and disputes with their
  supporting evidence; popularity is not consensus, and consensus is not fact.

- For papers, include the research question, methods, data, results, contributions, and limitations,
  distinguishing correlation from causation and results from author interpretations.

- Produce concise notes stating reading scope, key sources, and gaps, with supplementary material
  labeled separately; proceed when context suffices and ask only about missing information that
  changes verification.
